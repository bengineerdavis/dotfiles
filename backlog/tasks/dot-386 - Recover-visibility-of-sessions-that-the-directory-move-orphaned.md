## Description

<!-- SECTION:DESCRIPTION:BEGIN -->

The repos moved from `~/code/templates/` to `~/code/personal/templates/`. The
author reports that **other sessions are no longer visible** as a result, and
asks how to recover them.

This is a real loss of coordination, not a cosmetic one. Sessions are the
mechanism by which concurrent work is discovered; if they cannot be seen, two
sessions can edit the same files, claim the same task, or contradict each
other's decisions with no signal. The evidence that this already happened is in
this repo: `main` carries 22 unpushed commits from at least two sessions, and
the working tree holds modified files from sessions other than the one asking.

**What is known, measured 2026-11-19:**

- The move left a ghost tree at `~/code/templates/` containing only
  lore-generated `AGENTS.md` and `.lore.md` stubs — no `.git`, no code. It was
  deleted, then regenerated by sessions still parked there.
- Lore resolves a project from **cwd, not its DB row** (`hdrProject=absent` →
  `project=<cwd>`). Two project rows were repointed to the new paths, but a
  session whose cwd is the old path resolves to a project that no longer
  exists.
- Two live processes were found holding the ghost directory as cwd (an
  `opencode` PID and a `zsh`), which is why the stubs kept reappearing. They
  were deliberately not killed — they belong to other sessions.
- `~/.config/gita/repos.csv` still carries rows pointing at the deleted ghost
  tree; gita filters missing paths from every operation, including `gita rm`'s
  own choice list, so they cannot be pruned through the CLI.

**The question to answer:** where does session visibility actually come from,
and what did the move break? Candidates to check, in order:

- **Lore's project list** — `lore` project rows and their paths, and whether a
  session at a stale cwd is invisible or merely misattributed.
- **The backlog queue** — whether other sessions' claims and in-progress tasks
  are still discoverable, or whether their task files are the only trace.
- **OpenCode's own session store** — whether sessions are keyed by directory,
  and what happens to a session whose directory no longer exists.
- **The ghost tree** — whether any session is still parked there, and what
  happens to its work when the directory is finally gone.

Ship:

- **A determination of what "session visibility" means here** — which store
  holds it, and which part the move broke
- **A recovery path for sessions orphaned by the move**: how to find them, how
  to move them to the new path, and how to tell whether their work is already
  in `main` or still only in their working tree
- **A check for work stranded in the ghost tree or in sessions whose cwd no
  longer exists**, before anything is deleted
- **A durable fix** so a future move does not silently orphan sessions again —
  or a recorded decision that it will, and what to do when it does
- **The gita CSV rows resolved**, since they are the same failure class: a
  registration whose path vanished becomes unremovable

Constraints:

- **Do not kill other sessions' processes.** They are not this session's to
  end; the fix is to move or close them deliberately, by their owner.
- Do not delete the ghost tree again until it is confirmed no session is
  parked there — the previous deletion was followed by regeneration.
- Do not assume a session's work is lost because it is invisible. Check the
  working tree and `main` before concluding anything.
- This is a coordination problem, not a cleanup problem. The deliverable is
  restored visibility, not a tidier directory.
- Findings go to the author; other sessions' work is not this task's to
  reconcile.

Scoped loose. The author asked for this to be queued after the current work,
and the investigation decides whether the answer is a tool, a habit, or a
change to how sessions are started.

Decision 2026-11-19: sessions orphaned by the directory move must be recovered
and made visible, because invisible concurrent work is how two sessions
contradict each other.
